## Task and data

- Task: narrative coding formulated as "gated, typed decisions" — a fixed set of typed questions (presence / categorical / ordinal) put to a non-generative decision model (Jev) that returns, per question, an answer from a closed option set plus a probability, with no free text generated. "This paper formulates narrative coding as gated, typed decisions answered by Jev, a System One model that returns probabilities over analyst-defined options and generates no text." (Abstract) A "gate" means a dependent/detail question (e.g., which drug class) is only interpreted if its presence question (e.g., drug_involved) exceeded threshold τ_g = 0.5; otherwise the detail question is forced to its no-match option (Eq. 1, Section 3.2).
- Corpus: Texas CRIS crash-narrative file 2017–2025, 5,109,746 crash records; after dropping empty/≤40-character narratives, 5,018,080 narratives form the "analyzed corpus." Median narrative length 378 characters (p95 935, p99 1427). (Section 3.1, Table 2)
- Schema: 27 questions — 16 presence, 8 categorical, 3 ordinal, 7 gated. Full text of every question reproduced in Table A.1 (Appendix A). ("The schema holds 27 questions, of which 16 are presence questions, 8 are categorical, 3 are ordinal and 7 are gated" — Section 3.2)
- Two-stage coding:
  - Stage 1 screen: lean 10-presence-question schema run over a random frame of 500,000 narratives; 499,500 returned an answer; cost $25.23. ("In the first stage a lean schema of 10 presence questions is run over a random frame of 500,000 narratives, of which 499,500 returned an answer, at a cost of $25.23." — Section 3.2)
  - Stage 2 full coding: full 27-question schema run over 195,857 narratives (150,000 random-stratum "inferential backbone" + a rare-class-enriched part), at a cost of $30.22, with **no errors** ("The second stage coded 195,857 narratives with no errors at a cost of $30.22." — Section 3.2).
- Cost-governed-by-schema-size claim: "Cost is governed by schema size rather than narrative length." (Abstract) Quantitatively: fitted token model T(k,ℓ) = α + βk + bℓ with β=122 tokens/question and b=0.236 tokens/char (R²=0.987 for the question-count model); at 27 questions the schema contributes ~3,451 tokens per call vs. ~89 tokens for a median 378-character narrative. ("Each additional question costs a constant 122 tokens regardless of the corpus... asking more questions in one call is the dominant efficiency lever." — Section 4.1)

## Decision model and frontier comparators

- Decision model: **Jev**, version string **jev-1.13.0** (Table 2: "Model | jev-1.13.0 (195,857)"; also written "Jev 1.13" in Tables 5/9/11). Developer: TypeSafe, described as a "System One model" (TypeSafe, 2026a/2026c), released September 2026. Priced at $0.042 per million input tokens, no charge for output (per developer documentation, cited not verified independently). All runs use a pinned model identifier recorded per call.
- Two frontier generative-model comparators, run on the same 2,465 narrative-variable pairs / 400 narratives and scored against the same human labels with the same weights and metric code (Table 9, "Jev against frontier generative models on the same human reference labels"):
  - **claude-fable-5-1** — F1 0.967, precision 0.959, recall 0.976, ECE 0.0282, Brier 0.0025, calibration slope 2.55, Spiegelhalter z = -6.0, elicited (verbalized) probability, 87 distinct probability values elicited. This is "the first frontier arm" in prose, which "fails in the same direction as the typed model" (over-states prevalence).
  - **gpt-5.6-sol** — F1 0.885, precision 0.970, recall 0.814, ECE 0.0027, Brier 0.0056, calibration slope 0.98, z = -0.3, elicited, 79 distinct values elicited. This is "the second frontier arm," the best-calibrated of the three models tested.
- Prompting / tuning-budget parity: both frontier arms "received the criteria text verbatim and the same per-narrative variable assignment, and nothing else" — i.e., the identical schema criteria text the typed model and the human coders received, no fine-tuning, no few-shot examples. "Each arm was therefore asked to state a probability and to use the full range, which makes the elicited number a verbalized confidence rather than a read-out of the model's internals." They ran "through an agent harness in 20 batches of 20 narratives, with all criteria in context and no constraint on intermediate reasoning, so they had more context and more inference-time computation per judgment than the typed model." Their cost was not metered by the harness (Section 3.3, Section 4.4). No arm was held out from the reference set; all three (Jev + 2 frontier) were scored on the same 2,416 usable human judgments (Table 9 note).

## Reference sets ("coded fields" vs. blinded human judgments)

1. **Coded CRIS fields** (administrative reference): 5,601,890 crashes aggregated to crash level via the mapping in Table B.2, giving 9 presence variables a coded counterpart (alcohol, drug, fatigue, animal, phone, unbelted, hydroplane [proxy], wrong-way, medical episode). This reference is explicitly framed as incomplete/one-sided (never records a factor it shouldn't, but omits factors the narrative states). reference_label_type = **administrative_field**.
2. **Human reference set** (blinded human judgments): reference_label_type = **human_adjudicated**.
   - Sampling design (Section 3.3): each variable's returned probability was cut into 5 cells — [0,0.05), [0.05,0.3), [0.3,0.7), [0.7,0.95), [0.95,1] — and narratives were drawn **sequentially from the second-stage random stratum** (150,000) by a greedy rule that repeatedly picked the narrative filling the most still-short cells, targeting 30/40/40/40/30 per cell. A narrative drawn for one variable is labeled for *all* its "active" variables (p ≥ 0.05) plus 3 randomly chosen low-probability variables as negative controls.
   - 640 candidates were drawn; 137 were withheld by the PII/identifier screen (Section 3.1); the first 400 that cleared the screen were labeled.
   - 3 coders with graduate training in transportation safety, recruited to three different profiles, labeled 2,465 narrative-variable pairs (5,024 judgments total). 2,371 pairs double-coded, 94 triple-coded. Pairwise inter-coder Cohen's kappa on the shared calibration block ran 0.95–1.00 (median 0.95); raw agreement 99.0%; median judgment took 7.4 seconds.
   - Ties/disputes/unclear pairs dropped: 48 disputed + 18 unclear = 49 excluded, leaving **2,416 usable judgments** — the number used for every headline accuracy/calibration statistic in Section 4.3/4.4.
   - Weighting: because the draw was greedy + screened (not a probability sample), weights are **calibration weights** (Deville & Särndal 1992 sense), not inverse-inclusion-probability (Horvitz–Thompson) weights. Weights span a factor of 189; design effect 1.95, so the 2,416 usable judgments "carry the information of about 1,265."
   - Intervals: cluster bootstrap resampling **narratives** (not judgments) within the 5 draw routes, B = 1,000 replicates, bias-recentered percentile interval (Eq. 6). No explicit "95%" confidence level is stated for these bootstrap intervals anywhere in the body text (only Table 12's crash-attribution intervals are explicitly labeled "95% interval").
3. Both comparisons are reported with the ledger's reference_label_type distinction: coded-field comparisons = **administrative_field** (agreement measure, "bounded by the reference's own error"); human-judgment comparisons = **human_adjudicated** (the paper's only accuracy claims, Section 4.3: "This subsection carries the paper's only accuracy claim, and every other number in this section is agreement with coded fields.").

## Headline metrics and n

- Pooled F1 vs. human labels: **0.908** [0.871, 0.936], n = 2,416; precision 0.902 [0.877, 0.926]; recall 0.915; weighted Cohen's kappa 0.906. 11/16 variables reach F1 ≥ 0.85. Four variables fall below F1 0.70: unbelted (0.45), intentional (0.49), wrong_way (0.52), vehicle_defect (0.68/0.69).
- Against coded fields (n = 150,000 per variable, 9 variables with a coded counterpart), calibration error (ECE, exact grid estimator) ranges 0.012 (unbelted) to 0.045 (wrong_way); calibration slope 0.51–1.24.
- test_set_exposure: **new** — this is a purpose-built, newly drawn (2026) reference set on a private state administrative corpus held under a data-use agreement; not a public pre-2026 benchmark.

## Cost and latency

- Realized cost: Stage-2 (full 27-question schema, 195,857 narratives) = **$30.22 total**, i.e., **$0.1543 per 1,000 narratives**. Stage-1 screen (10-question schema, 500,000-narrative frame) = $25.23. Token-model calibration sweep (7 question counts, 420 calls) = $0.03. **Total spend across every model call reported in the paper = $55.79.**
- Projected whole-corpus cost: coding all 5,018,080 narratives at the realized Stage-2 rate would cost **$774** (the whole corpus itself was never coded).
- Mid-tier frontier scenario (list-price, not the two benchmarked arms — their cost was not metered): $7.95 per 1,000 narratives, a factor of 52 above Jev's $0.154; projected whole-corpus cost **≈$39,875**; largest priced tier a factor of 129, smallest a factor of 26. This scenario is explicitly NOT the same as the two frontier arms actually benchmarked for accuracy (Table 9) — "The scenario prices tiers rather than the arms of Table 9, and the two must not be read as one curve." Seed figures $774 vs $39,875 are both **verified verbatim**.
- Latency: median 0.20 s per call, p90 0.33 s, sustained throughput ~44 requests/second, mean 3,673 input tokens/narrative, 0 failed calls (Table 2, Section 4.1). Concurrency 10 with exponential backoff (≤5 attempts). Hardware/provider: TypeSafe Jev API (hosted; no self-hosted hardware specs given). Prices are "TypeSafe list prices accessed 2026-09-17."

## Calibration analysis

- Pooled raw ECE (against human reference, exact discrete-grid estimator Eq. 12) = **0.0231** [0.0212, 0.0244/0.0245], Brier 0.0045 (skill score 0.828), Spiegelhalter z = −5.3 (bootstrap SE 0.23), calibration slope **1.63** (>1, meaning probabilities are "not extreme enough" — model hedges, opposite of typical neural-net over-confidence), intercept −0.82. Mean probability 4.8% vs. weighted base rate 2.7% (~80% relative over-statement).
- Binning method for the headline ECE: the paper uses its own **exact/discrete-grid estimator** (Eq. 12, "binning-free," grouped by the model's own attainable 2-decimal grid values) rather than conventional equal-mass/equal-width binned ECE (Eq. 11). "Every calibration error reported against either reference in this paper is the exact grid error of Eq. (12) rather than the binned error of Eq. (11)." The coded-field table (Table 4) tabulates the equal-mass estimator computed upstream but the two "agree at the printed precision except for one variable."
- Recalibration (Table 6, Section 4.2, Eq. 20): **Platt scaling** ("logistic on the logit", 2-parameter, unpenalized intercept+slope) and **isotonic regression** are both explicitly fitted and reported — fitted on one half of the narratives, scored on the other, both directions, 50 repeats. Held-out pooled ECE falls from 0.0231 (raw) to **0.0069 under Platt** [0.0045, 0.0112] and **0.0069 under isotonic** [0.0045, 0.0111] — "a factor of 3.3." Post-Platt slope 0.97 (vs. 1.63 raw), intercept −0.06. **Both methods are explicitly named**, contrary to the possibility flagged in the task brief that only "recalibration" (unnamed) appears; the method-naming happens in Section 3.4 (Eq. 20) and Table 6, not just the abstract.
- Per-variable recalibration (11/16 variables with ≥20 positive human labels get their own Platt map; isotonic is not broken out per-variable in Table 6, only pooled): held-out per-variable Platt ECE ranges 0.0007–0.0339, median 0.0034, vs. raw median 0.0261.

## Resolution-floor bound and review-budget-per-variable-per-year

- **Resolution floor** (Proposition 1, Section 3.4 & Appendix D): because Jev returns probabilities on a discrete 2-decimal grid (values 0.01–1.00 in steps of 0.01; across 2.4M answers on the random stratum the value 0 never occurred and 1 occurred twice, so w₀≈0), if a variable's true base rate ρ is below the grid's smallest positive value δ=0.01, "no allocation of grid values is calibrated," and ECE_G ≥ δ(1−w₀) − ρ > 0 — i.e., the mean probability necessarily over-states prevalence, independent of model skill. Applies to 4 of the 9 coded-reference variables whose base rate < 0.01 (drug_involved, phone_use, wrong_way, medical_episode); the bound on attainable ECE for these runs **0.0046 to 0.0057, maximum for phone_use**.
- **Review budget over flagged records** H(π*) (Eq. 18, Section 3.4/4.3): because accuracy-based selective risk is vacuous for rare positive classes (a "flag nothing" rule already beats conventional 1–5% risk targets), the paper instead defines the human-review workload as the share of *flagged* records that cannot be auto-accepted at a target precision π* (default 90%). Certified out-of-fold (threshold chosen on one narrative half, scored on the other, 100 splits). Pooled held-out H(0.90) = **0.8%** of flagged records (vs. 0.0% in-sample — the "optimism of in-sample selection"), delivering out-of-fold precision 0.906 (lower confidence bound 0.844, approximate). Against the coded reference the budget is far worse: 8 of 9 coded-comparable variables require reviewing essentially every flagged record (budget saturates), only animal_involved can auto-accept most flags; implied Texas workload = 68,049 variable-level review decisions/year (20,262 for alcohol alone), or 61,994 narratives/year (11.1% of the corpus) as document-level "union of flagged sets."

## Kappa-understatement finding

- "Agreement with coded fields understates fidelity to the narrative by a median of 0.26 in kappa" (abstract). Exact statement and numbers (Section 4.2): "Cohen's kappa against the coded field is below the calibration-weighted kappa against human judgment for all 9 of 9 variables that carry a coded counterpart, by a **median of 0.26**. The widest case is drug involved, which reaches **0.48** against the coded field and **0.96** against human judgment... The narrowest agreement of all is unbelted at **0.10** against the coded field, and there the human reference agrees that the model is weak, at **0.45**." Measured by comparing Table 10's coded-field kappa (n=150,000 per variable) against Table 5's calibration-weighted kappa vs. the human reference set (n=2,416 total, variable-specific n's of ~107–206). Mechanism: Lemma 2 ("one-sided reference noise") — the coded field never records a factor the narrative does not state but frequently omits factors the narrative does state, so a correct extraction reads as a false positive against the coded field without moving the model's calibration.

## The "10,747 per year" attribution finding

- Context (Section 4.5, computed on the Stage-2 random stratum containing 44,403 injury or fatal crashes, of which 964 are fatal, scaled to Texas per year): "The coded fields attribute **28,057** injury and fatal crashes per year across the nine factors, and the narrative variables add **10,747** more." The added count is not a raw model-flag count but the expected number of coded-negative crashes whose narrative states the factor, obtained by **summing the recalibrated (Platt-mapped) probability** over those crashes — "Summing a calibrated probability rather than counting model flags is what makes the second term an estimate of a population total." Relative increase by factor ranges 16% (unbelted) to 182% (phone_use), median 39%; 4 of 9 factors change rank. Largest absolute addition: alcohol_involved, +1,784/year [1,530, 2,649] 95% interval. Confirmation of narrative-only flags (by a coder) ranges from 0.15 (unbelted, weak) to 0.90 (phone_use, strong) — used to discipline which additions "can be believed" rather than to compute the totals.

## Paper's own stated limitations (Conclusion, "boundaries" paragraph — quoted verbatim)

"The study has boundaries that the reader should hold in mind, and this paragraph states them. The model is closed and commercial, its training data are undisclosed, and its behavior may change without notice. The pinned identifier and the recorded per-call identifier make such a change detectable but not preventable, so a reader cannot rerun the analysis if the vendor withdraws the model, and audits of commercial systems should be treated as perishable. The coded CRIS fields used as the large reference are incomplete, so every statistic computed against them is an agreement measure bounded by the reference's own error. The accuracy claims rest on a human reference set that is smaller by three orders of magnitude and that is defined only on cases the coders found clear, because disputes were excluded rather than adjudicated. Its pairwise reliability is estimated on 94 triple-coded pairs, and its stratification on the model's probability rather than on the positive class leaves the rarest variables with a handful of positive labels, as few as 5 for 2 of the 4 that fail. Per-variable precision, any threshold fitted to it, and the calibration error at the floor stratum are correspondingly imprecise for those variables, and 5 variables carry too few positives to support a recalibration map of their own. The weights that make the reference set speak for the population are calibration weights rather than known inclusion probabilities, because the frame was drawn by a greedy cell-filling rule and then screened. Their validity therefore rests on labeling being uninformative about the outcome within a cell. The recalibration maps were fitted on 3 coders' judgment of Texas narratives and should be refitted rather than transported. The downstream counts of Section 4.5 inherit every one of these boundaries, and for the 2 factors whose narrative-only flags the coders confirmed less than half the time they should be read as an upper bound rather than as an estimate. The frontier arms ran with more context and more computation per judgment than the typed model, and their cost was not metered at all, so no cost in this paper describes them and the price comparison is a scenario over commercially listed tiers. The analysis covers one state, narratives are written by officers with agency-specific conventions and are themselves incomplete records of the crash, and residual identifiers survive the data owner's de-identification pass at the rates reported in Section 3.1, which is why no narrative is reproduced here." (Section 6, Conclusion)

- Note: the paper places most "limitations" content only in the Conclusion's boundaries paragraph, plus a shorter "Transfer is a hypothesis" paragraph in Section 5 (Discussion) that lists conditions under which the cost model, schema, recalibration map, and accuracy results would fail to transfer to another state/model version.
