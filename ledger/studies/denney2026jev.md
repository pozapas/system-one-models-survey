# denney2026jev

arXiv 2610.06625 (v2, 06 Oct 2026; first public version dated 2026-10-05). "JEV versus LLMs: Accuracy, Cost and Calibration on Seven Political Science Replications". Full text: fulltext/2610.06625.html and .txt. Extractor batch V08.

**Authors and affiliation.** Steven Denney (Leiden Institute for Area Studies, Leiden University) and Matthew DiGiuseppe (Institute of Political Science, Leiden University) (title page). Academic.

**Task and data.** Seven published political-science studies are re-run with Jev (Table 1): tweet relevance and frames (Gilardi et al. 2023; 2,403 agreed 2021 tweets, 411 2023 tweets), tweet sentiment (Ornstein et al. 2025; 907 of 945 tweets), terrorist attack type (Brandt et al. 2026; 37,709 GTD incidents 2017 to 2020; 322 BBC articles), V-Dem indicators from country names (Weidmann et al. 2026; 9,041 country-indicator pairs, 53 indicators, 171 countries, 2023), European party positions (Di Leo et al. 2025; 347,801 dyads), survey-answer scaling (DiGiuseppe and Flynn 2026; 28,040 and 30,280 pairs, 243 expert-judged pairs with 460 judgments), manifestos and speeches (Le Mens and Gallego 2025; 18,263 and 429 sentences). Four extra calibration tasks (WiscAds 1,944 pairs, Immigration 3,265, Park 2,228, EU-sentence screening 121,877 sentences) are in Appendix H/I (Figure 2 caption; Appendix "Additional calibration tasks"). Prompts and wording are kept from the originals with reply-format lines removed for Jev (Section "How does JEV compare", Appendix A).

**Decision models.** Jev, requested as typesafe/jev-1.13 through OpenRouter on 19, 22 and 23 September 2026; every response reported typesafe/jev-1.13-20260917, taken as jev-1.13.0 (Appendix E). V-Dem run through TypeSafe's own endpoint (Section "How does JEV compare"). Output types Choice, Score, Noul (Section "What JEV returns"). Architecture unknown to the authors (Appendix E).

**Comparators.** GPT-6 Luna called directly at OpenAI (Batch API on 25 and 28 September 2026, temperature 0, fixed seed, reasoning effort none; published prompts sent verbatim including reply-format lines) (Appendix H). Qwen3.8-27B via OpenRouter on one host (Parasail, FP8), reasoning off, temperature 0, one pass, run 30 September 2026 (Table 10, Appendix F). Published models from the original papers (GPT-3.5, GPT-4, GPT-4o, Llama, ConfliBERT, ConflLlama and others) compared against published figures. Tuning-budget parity: same published prompt and wording for all systems; no tuning; Jev is run on a prompt adapted to Choice/Score/Noul (descriptions of options written by the authors, Appendix C); an asymmetry is that Jev judges pairs in both orders while Luna and Qwen judge the published order only (except expert pairs) (Section Open-ended survey responses).

**Reference labels.** Trained research-assistant agreement (human, two coders, disagreements dropped) for Gilardi (Appendix C); mean of three experts after a second round of recoding (Fleiss kappa 0.72) for sentiment (Section Tweet sentiment); GTD coders' first attack type (database coding by START researchers following the codebook, adjudication not described) (Section Terrorist attack type); V-Dem expert codes from a measurement model over a median of six coders (Section Democracy indicators); CHES, CMP and TEV placements; 23 raters on 460 pair judgments (raters agree with each other on same pair 75.2% of the time, Appendix G); expert coding (manifestos) and crowd coding (speeches) (Section Manifestos and speeches).

**n, design and intervals.** Sizes above. Paired bootstrap for differences (Efron 1979; Tables 9 and 10), 2,000 resamples for sentiment and expert pairs, country bootstrap for V-Dem, Fisher intervals for party correlations (Table 13). One run per system except four Jev runs for Gilardi (Appendix C) and replays (Appendix B). Some differences depend on the bootstrap seed (Table 1 footnote a).

**Test-data exposure.** Recall tasks (V-Dem v14, CHES, TEV, CMP) are public before the models' training; the authors state high agreement may reflect recall of the benchmarks and that Jev, Luna and Qwen were trained after V-Dem v14 release (Section Party positions/Democracy indicators). TypeSafe's CEO told TechCrunch training data are exclusively synthetic; the authors note that does not rule out exposure (Appendix E). Tweets and GTD are public pre-2026. No contamination check beyond this discussion.

**Cost and latency.** Table 2 and Appendix H cost: the 510,681 Luna requests cost $7.17 and the Jev Gilardi four-run collection cost $0.61 (Appendix C), reported as measured costs per run. Table 2: dollars per 1,000 decisions, e.g. Gilardi 0.017 (Jev) vs 0.017 (Luna Batch) vs 0.034 (Luna Standard) vs 0.085 (Qwen). Luna price per decision 0.45 to 1.03 times Jev's across 10 matched tasks (Appendix H); Jev bills 1.2 to 3.3 times as many input tokens as Luna (Introduction). Qwen 2.35 to 5.41 times Jev's cost (Section on speed and cost). Latency 0.09 to 0.27 s per decision for Jev vs 0.75 to 1.04 s Luna and 0.39 to 0.87 s Qwen, one request at a time, 200 timed items per application, from the Netherlands on 29 to 30 September 2026 (Table 2 caption). Throughput 45 to 156 decisions per second for Jev with 16 requests at once (Table 2).

**Calibration.** ECE and AUROC against human labels over ten probability bins (the binning scheme is not stated; the ledger codes calibration_quantity as ECE-equal-width because the paper cites Guo et al. 2017 for ECE), 95% bootstrap intervals, plotted in Figure 2 (values mostly figure-only). Table 9 gives V-Dem calibration error Jev 0.057 vs Luna 0.390; Table 10 Qwen 0.152; survey calibration Jev 0.109, Luna 0.114. Appendix B single-call pairwise ECE Jev 0.071, Luna 0.145, Qwen 0.011. Luna has higher ECE than Jev on all eight categorical reading tasks, but the gap disappears on survey pairs averaged over orders and reverses on attack type (Section JEV's advantage?). No post-hoc recalibration reported. Jev probabilities rounded to two decimals; 14.8% of Choice/Score probabilities exactly zero (Appendix E).

**Cascade or escalation.** None run; the paper mentions routing uncertain answers to a human or stronger LLM as a use of probabilities but does not test it (Section JEV's advantage?).

**Option-name, cardinality, order and robustness.** Nine-way Choice vs nine separate Noul questions on GTD (macro F1 0.413 for one-question-per-type vs 0.555, Table 11); category names only vs codebook (Table 11). Pair order: Jev leans to second slot in Study 1 (bias -0.0358), flip rate 8.8% and 8.5% for the two studies; forward and reverse scales correlate 0.982 (Appendix B). Replay: same winner in 98.6% of 493 identical pairwise requests; probability vector identical in only 40.7% of 1,000 replays (Appendices B and E). Transitivity 98.18% on sparse triplets (Appendix B).

**Code and data availability.** The text points to the authors' files by path (data/batch_inputs/lemens_prompts_used.json, Appendix A) but gives no repository URL and the HTML links no archive; recorded as partial.

**Authors' own limitations and cautions.** Task is easy for relevance (Appendix C); Jev architecture unknown so cost and speed claims cannot be verified; timings from one location on two days (Section on speed and cost); recall tasks may be contaminated; framing instructions reconstructed; raters disagree with one another; comparison to published models confounded by model age and quantisation (Appendix F); some intervals depend on bootstrap seed. Conclusion: Jev is on par with mid-tier LLMs, faster, no clear cost advantage at batch prices, calibration better than Luna's token probabilities but not consistently better than Qwen's (Abstract, Conclusion).
