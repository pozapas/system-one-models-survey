# teke2026ufakzeka

arXiv 2610.06744 (v1, 05 Oct 2026). "ufakzeka-karar: An Open Turkish Typed-Decision Model with Order-Invariant Option Scoring". Full text: fulltext/2610.06744.html and .txt. Extractor batch V08.

**Author and affiliation.** Sait Furkan Teke, ufak AI (title page). Industry, single author, own-model paper.

**Task and data.** Turkish typed decisions (choice, ordered level, yes or no) evaluated on HakemBench v1.0, a Turkish typed-decision benchmark from a companion paper [2]. Open set 2,346 items, 4,275 questions, seven tracks; most gold settled by passes of AI models, 260 questions carry human-decided gold (Section 6). Also six external Turkish sets (MASSIVE 1.1 tr-TR 2,937; MiDe22 1,012; OffensEval-TR 2020 A 3,528; MMLU-Pro-TR 11,838; XCOPA 500; X-FACT Turkish 169) (Table 3).

**Decision models.** ufakzeka-karar: 182,494,466 parameters, built on ufakzeka-1-base, order-invariant option scoring head, temperature-scaled probabilities plus an isotonic "not sure" value, one CPU forward pass (Abstract, Sections 2 and 5). Three runs scored: Run 1 (42,998 training rows), Run 2 (55,870), Run 3 released (55,679), T=1.2614 for Run 3 (Table 1, Section 4). Released under Apache-2.0 (weights, code, result files) (Section 9). Board models queried 26 and 27 September 2026 UTC: Jev 1.13, Kev 4B and 9B, Laya variants, decider-2b, open-jev (DeBERTa-v3-large), Qwen3.5-4B, DeepSeek V4 Pro, Gemini 3.8 Flash, GPT-5.6 Sol, GLM 5.3, and a surface-cue baseline (Section 6, Table 2). Board numbers for other models are taken from the public board of the companion paper.

**Comparators and tuning parity.** Other models are scored by the benchmark organiser's protocol (zero-shot typed questions; each model's own calibration; DeepSeek V4 Pro read through option-label log-probabilities) (Sections 6). ufakzeka-karar is trained on in-distribution data for some tracks (court items and 28 guardrail items come from train splits that are in its training data) while others answer zero-shot (Section 6, By track). The author states Run 3 numbers are not blind: Run 1 and Run 2 results on the full test set were read, Run 2's data targeted Run 1 errors, and Run 3 protocol was fixed after reading Run 2's guardrail results; guardrail, moderation and customer support numbers carry the flag "shaped by reading the test results" (Abstract, Section 4). The benchmark companion paper's author team appears to be the same lab (benchmark is "a companion paper [2]", "we added" a surface-cue baseline, Table 2 caption).

**Reference labels.** AI-model passes for most HakemBench gold, 260 human-decided questions (Section 6). Support development set (255 questions) and a human check (84 questions) are answered by one person, the author, with no inter-annotator agreement (Section 3, Section 8). Training labels from LLM judges and rules (Section 8). Ledger uses model_consensus for HakemBench rows.

**n, sampling, intervals.** 95 percent percentile bootstrap intervals from 2,000 resamples over items, same for every model; items written in one generation request are correlated so intervals are likely too narrow (Sections 6 and 8). Three random seeds for training recipes. No intervals for several calibration changes (Section 5 Results).

**Test-data exposure.** HakemBench is new (September 2026) but its open items were visible to the author, and the released model is shaped by reading test results (Sections 4, 6). Training data was checked against 70 evaluation sets and all HakemBench v1.0 items by shared 8-grams and MinHash, flagged rows removed (Section 3). External sets are public before 2026; MASSIVE, MiDe22 and OffensEval are supervised, X-FACT claims partly from a source also used in training (Section 6, Table 3).

**Cost and latency.** Median 102.5 ms per question on an Apple M1 with one PyTorch thread (13 sample questions; 69.8 to 194.5 ms; 986,038,272 bytes peak memory, fp32) (Section 6, Probes and speed). Board median ms per question 103.6 versus Jev 1.13 135.0 on non-comparable setups (Table 2 caption). Training: 3,480 steps on one NVIDIA H200, 692.9 s (Section 4). The author's project spend is stated in Section 9 and is not extracted.

**Calibration.** Smooth ECE (kernel-smoothed; ledger codes calibration_quantity as none because it is not one of the schema binning types) and Brier. Temperature scaling selected among four forms on a hash-split fit/selection part of validation rows (Section 5, Method). Result: temperature raises smooth ECE on held-out support rows (Run 1 0.027 to 0.045; Run 3 0.036 to 0.064, not an unseen test) and lowers it on the support development set (0.050 to 0.038) (Table 1, Section 5). Guardrail track smooth ECE 0.158 versus 0.025 to 0.084 on other tracks (Section 6). Benchmark calibration axis 0.482 versus Jev 1.13 0.706 (Table 2).

**Cascade or escalation.** Not-sure signal: threshold 0.9 passes 0.292 of 4,275 open questions with 0.062 wrong; at 0.99, error 0.085 on 213 answers, with 18 of 148 guardrail answers wrong (Section 5). The signal cannot be relied on to catch guardrail errors; on customer support almost no answer reaches 0.9 (6 of 1,340).

**Option-name, order, cardinality, language, robustness.** Order sensitivity 0.000 by construction; sequential head trained with shuffled options changes 2.3 to 2.8 percent of answers under reordering (Table 4); paraphrase agreement 0.883 versus Jev 0.926 (Section 6); English Brier gap -0.303 [-0.377, -0.225] (not trained for English); up to 255 options via passes of ten (Sections 1, 2). REINFORCE loses 10.2 macro F1 points to cross-entropy; int8 quantisation loses more than 0.5 points so release is fp32 (Table 4).

**Code and data availability.** Weights, code, benchmark and demo links in Section 9 (https://github.com/ufakai/ufakzeka-karar; https://huggingface.co/ufakai/ufakzeka-karar; https://huggingface.co/datasets/ufakai/HakemBench).

**Authors' own limitations (Section 8).** Six board models score higher; support routing below surface-cue baseline on the human check; single annotator; calibration does not transfer everywhere; not-sure signal misses guardrail errors; numbers come after readings of earlier runs' test results; passes only 128 of 201 harmless look-alikes; moderation may be inflated by style likeness; weak world knowledge; Turkish only; text beyond 448 tokens cut; most labels from LLMs or rules; intervals likely too narrow.
