## Task and data

- **Core question**: "whether a decision-only judge can provide an economical first pass and identify when stronger evaluation is needed" (Abstract, line 109) — i.e., JEV (TypeSafe's hosted typed-decision model) used as an LLM-judge, benchmarked against generative LLM judges and reward models, with a downstream escalation/cascade policy.
- **What "derivation checking" means**: judgments that require verifying a multi-step solution process, not just the final answer — operationalized via JudgeBench's math/reasoning/coding items. The Appendix H case study (line 774) makes this concrete: a JudgeBench parachutist problem where "Response A selects the correct 345-meter option through a flawed derivation" and JEV is fooled (gives the wrong response 0.91 probability) while GPT-6 is not. "A correct final choice and a faithful derivation are distinct targets."
- **What "elaborately written wrong answer" means**: RM-Bench's style-adversarial condition, where "the rejected answer is the more elaborately written one" (line 228, Table 2 "Style-adversarial pairs" row) — i.e., a more verbose/confident-sounding wrong answer pitted against a plainer correct one. JEV's accuracy drops 9.2 points from matched-style to style-adversarial pairs (line 228); GPT-6's is essentially flat (+1.3 pts).
- **Full list of benchmarks/datasets used** (Section 3 "Public benchmarks" and "Existing data and controls", lines 147–159; Appendix A Table 4, line 404–412; Appendix E, line 610–631; Appendix J, line 818–824; Appendix K, line 875–892):
  1. **RewardBench** — 400 pairs (100 each from chat, difficult chat, safety, reasoning), after exact-duplicate removal (line 149).
  2. **JudgeBench** — complete 350-pair GPT-4o split (knowledge/reasoning/mathematics/coding); Claude split not used (line 149).
  3. **HaluEval** — 240 evidence-grounded judgments: 120 QA questions each with reference + hallucinated answer, judged against supplied evidence (line 149).
  4. **"Existing labels"** — 150 saved replies from the authors' own prior multi-turn answer-consistency experiments (six generator models; neutral/supportive/challenging/reference-informed follow-ups), used for final-answer adjudication (line 153).
  5. **GSM8K trajectories control** — 108 final replies from twelve nine-round GSM8K conversations with Qwen3-32B repeatedly challenged, all reference-correct (positive control) (line 153, 532).
  6. **Synthetic EVIDENCE control** — 64 synthetic evidence judgments (support/contradiction/missing-info/distractor) from sixteen families (line 153).
  7. **RewardBench 2** — 100 four-way selection prompts (20 per non-Ties category), frozen follow-up (Appendix E, line 610–616).
  8. **RM-Bench** — 80 sources × 3 styles × 2 orders = 1,440 judgments (16 per domain: chat/code/math/safety-refuse/safety-response), split into hard/normal/easy conditions (Appendix E, line 616).
  9. **Answer-format follow-up (Appendix J)**: the 150 existing replies re-scored under gold-blind extraction (4-way contract), plus 480 constructed direct judgments and 240 constructed extractions from 40 frozen HaluEval questions across 6 answer-commitment templates.
  10. **Natural free-response follow-up (Appendix K)**: 80 document-grounded HaluEval summaries (40 documents × original/hallucinated) and 80 reference-free general responses (balanced over existing hallucination labels), using HaluEval's own data/provenance (Li et al., 2023).
- **What was frozen and when** (Section 3, line 155–159; also Appendix A "Data partitions" Table 4, line 404–412): a 642-item pilot was frozen before any inference and split 40/60 by source question into a **selection set** (used only to fit temperatures and routing thresholds: 64 RewardBench + 32 JudgeBench pairs for preference thresholds, 32 HaluEval judgments for temperature) and a **pilot test set**. A disjoint **670-item extension** was frozen before pilot accuracy was inspected and "never touched a fit." "The initial window evaluated JEV, three GPT baselines, and PairRM; the expansion reran JEV and added model families on all 1,312 items after those results were known, so the extension is held out but the multi-family comparison is exploratory."

## Reference labels

- Every accuracy figure in the paper's primary results is "agreement with a benchmark's supplied label" (line 786) — i.e., **vendor_label** (RewardBench/JudgeBench/HaluEval/RM-Bench/RewardBench2 gold, "kept as supplied," line 149) for all primary numbers.
- **Blinded human adjudication (Appendix I, line 784–816)**: to check whether the label-based JEV-vs-GPT-6 gaps reflect real judge errors or label noise, the team adjudicated — blind to labels — **every base-order public item on which JEV and GPT-6 Astra's correctness differs**: RewardBench 400, JudgeBench 350, HaluEval 240 (expansion window, GPT-6 Astra). "The protocol was frozen before any annotation and is released with its amendment log, packet, returned sheets, and analysis code (Appendix L)" (line 786).
  - **n of adjudicated items = 183** (from 180 source clusters), stratified: S1 "exactly one judge matches the label" (108 items: RewardBench 29, JudgeBench 69, HaluEval 10, taken in full), S2 "neither judge matches the label" (55: 14/15/26, taken in full), S3 "both match, sampled by hash as a control" (20: 7/7/6) (line 798).
  - **Blinding**: annotators saw "an opaque packet id, the task and its broad domain, the question, the evidence (HaluEval), and the candidate responses, with pairwise responses relabeled Response 1/2 in an order swapped by hash; they saw no label, judge output, stratum, or original identifier" (line 798). Allowed: reference material, calculators, local code. Not allowed: LLM assistants, the JEV service, or the benchmarks' label files.
  - **Annotators/adjudication procedure**: "A member of the research team, aware of the study's purpose but not of item-level labels, completed all 183 items." The protocol called for a second human pass; instead a **second complete pass was produced by an LLM rater (Claude)** under the same blinding — "the amendment log records as a deviation." That LLM pass served only to flag which items needed adjudication (agreement with the human on the collapsed decision: 58.0%/83.5%/92.9% on RB/JB/HA, κ=0.29/0.74/0.81). The **39 items where the two passes disagreed** were decided by **an author** "who had seen the aggregate pre-adjudication results but no item-level key information" (line 800–802). So the final label per item is either the human annotator's (where the LLM pass agreed) or the adjudicating author's (otherwise); 36 of 183 final labels are "indecisive." This is explicitly **not** independent double-human annotation.
  - **Sampling design**: stratified by disagreement type (S1/S2/S3 above), not a random audit; S3 exists only as a control stratum sampled by hash.

## Calibration analysis

- JEV's **native confidence** is "a statistic of its distribution" (line 167) whose implementation the provider does not publish. The paper instead uses **q = max_k p_k (maximum label probability)** as its primary confidence signal throughout, justified because native confidence correlates with q at Spearman 0.971/0.999/0.948 on RewardBench/JudgeBench/HaluEval (line 167). Appendix G reports native confidence's own error-detection AUROC (0.870/0.745/0.865, line 762) is close to q's.
- **Low-confidence decisions are defined by binning on q** (Section 7 "Confidence orders the errors", line 304–306): "Figure 5 bins the 990 base-order judgments by JEV's maximum label probability q." Pooled across the three tasks, JEV is right on only **47.7% of the 65 items with q < 0.6**, rising to 76.5% (n=85, q∈[0.7,0.8)), 93.9% (n=147, q∈[0.95,0.99)), and 99.1% (n=322, q=1). GPT-6 on the *same* low-confidence items only drops from 99.1% to 78.5%, "so its advantage sits where JEV is unsure" — this is the textual basis for the seed claim "gap concentrated in low-confidence decisions." At q≥0.9 the two judges are "nearly interchangeable" (95.8% vs 96.5%); on the items JEV would escalate, GPT-6 leads by 15 points (67.9% vs 82.6%).
- **Calibration metrics reported**: Brier score, clipped NLL (floor 10⁻⁶), 10-bin ECE, error-detection AUROC (line 183). JEV's Brier scores: 0.111 (RewardBench), 0.297 (JudgeBench), 0.176 (HaluEval); GPT-6's: 0.104, 0.095, 0.245 (line 276) — "GPT-6 is both more accurate and better calibrated on the hard comparisons, yet its JudgeBench strength does not carry over to evidence-grounded uncertainty, where JEV's probabilities are the better ones."
- **Post-hoc temperature scaling** (line 282, Table 12): fitted on the pilot selection set only, tested on the disjoint extension. Result: "transfers unevenly" — NLL improves on HaluEval (0.333→0.284) but *worsens* on JudgeBench (0.449→0.475) and RewardBench (0.205→0.232); fitted temperatures point opposite directions (0.65 sharpens RewardBench; 2.15/4.45 soften JudgeBench/HaluEval). "No single temperature fits; each workload needs its own validation."
- **Where the confidence signal breaks down** (Section 7 "Where the signal weakens", line 324–326): on RM-Bench hard (style-adversarial) pairs, AUROC of q vs. correctness drops to 0.770 (vs. 0.918 easy, 0.902 normal); "JEV is wrong on a third of the pairs it scores in [0.9, 0.95) and on 15% of those in [0.95, 0.99)." On reference-free prose, AUROC is 0.518 (chance) and "no threshold helps."

## Cascade / frozen escalation result (full detail)

- **Two distinct cascade analyses** are reported and the paper is careful to separate them:
  1. **Single-order cascades (Section 7 "Single-order cascades," line 308–310; Table 13, Appendix F)**: post-hoc, thresholds "read off the items they are scored on" — explicitly *not* pre-specified. At pooled τ=0.9: escalates 34.3% of items, cascade accuracy 91.3% vs. GPT-6 alone 91.7% (99.6% retained), fee ratio 0.47 (0.44 conservative).
  2. **Frozen two-order policies (Section 7 "Frozen two-order policies," line 312–322; Table 3 main text, Table 11 Appendix F — full list of 9 fallbacks)**: this is the **pre-specified, headline result**. For pairwise preference judgments, JEV is queried in **both candidate orders** and the aligned probability of semantic response A is averaged (Eq. 1, line 314–318); exact ties get half-credit. "For each fallback, the threshold was chosen on the 96 pilot selection pairs (64 RewardBench, 32 JudgeBench) to maximize coverage while keeping selection-set accuracy within two points of the fallback... invalid first-stage outputs always defer. Grid and rule were fixed before the model expansion, and the pilot test set and extension serve only for evaluation." (line 320) — **this is the "frozen on what data" answer: the threshold-selection rule and grid were frozen on the 96-pair pilot *selection* set, before the model expansion (i.e., before GPT-5.4/5.6/6, Claude, Gemini, Qwen3.6/3.8 were added) and before any extension-set inference, specifically to avoid leakage into the 510-pair evaluation set.**
  - **Evaluated on**: 510 disjoint extension preference pairs (RewardBench + JudgeBench extension items), held out from all fitting.
  - **Headline number** (GPT-6 Astra fallback, τ=0.9, Table 3): "At threshold 0.9 the GPT-6 cascade accepts 53.7% of the 510 extension pairs, scores 92.5% against 93.1%, and uses 56.8% of the fallback's fee (62.2% under the conservative bound); the paired change is −0.59 points ([−1.78, 0.59])." (line 322) — i.e., cascade accuracy 92.5% retains **92.5/93.1 = 99.36%** of GPT-6 Astra's standalone accuracy, at **56.8%** of its fee (reported usage) or 62.2% (conservative upper bound).
  - **Conclusion's rounded headline** (line 330): "the escalation can be automatic: accept when confident, escalate when unsure, and keep **99% of GPT-6's accuracy at 57% of its fee**." This matches the GPT-6 Astra/τ=0.9 row above (99.36% ≈ "99%"; 56.8% ≈ "57%").
  - **Other fallbacks tried** (Table 3 + Appendix Table 11, 9 total): GPT-5.4 (τ=0.9, accept 53.7%, cascade 91.4% vs. fallback 91.6%, fee 0.639/1.096); GPT-5.6 Sol (τ=0.7, accept 81.0%, cascade 91.0% vs. fallback 93.3%, **loses 2.35 points, beyond its stated 2-point tolerance — "does not always transfer"**); GPT-6 Astra (τ=0.9, above); GPT-OSS 120B, Qwen3.6 27B, Qwen3.8 27B (all τ=0.5, "accept every pair and make no fallback call" — degenerate policies that beat their weak fallbacks just by order-averaging); Claude Sonnet 5, Gemini 3 Flash, Gemini 3.1 Pro at their own thresholds.
- **cascade_result summary for the ledger**: frozen two-order policy, GPT-6 Astra fallback, τ=0.9 → 92.5% cascade accuracy vs. 93.1% fallback-alone (99.4% retained) at 56.8%/62.2% of fallback fee; threshold frozen on 96-pair pilot selection set before model expansion/extension inference to prevent leakage.

## Option-name / robustness stress test

- **No dedicated option-name-swap or rubric-relabeling stress test is run in this paper** (unlike, e.g., Sun & Xu 2026's "Type-Safe Is Not Error-Free," a companion paper in this batch). What this paper *does* run instead, under "Stability and probability quality" (Section 6, line 268–286):
  - **Reversed candidate order** (position/order robustness, not option-naming): reversal changes 3.25% of RewardBench decisions and 11.14% of JudgeBench decisions for JEV; both-orders-correct accuracy 91.0%/74.0%. JEV's first-position pick rate is 48.9%/48.4% (i.e., no aggregate positional bias, but real per-pair inconsistency: of 39 inconsistent JudgeBench pairs, 14 always-first and 25 always-second).
  - **Repeated identical requests**: 0/96 decisions changed across repetitions.
  - **Paraphrased rubric wording**: 4/48 decisions changed.
  - **Equivalent interface primitives** (Choice vs. Noul vs. Score) can disagree by ~0.05 mean probability gap even holding the same underlying judgment (Section 6 "Equivalent interfaces can disagree," line 284–286; Appendix G).
  - None of this is an "option name" (label-wording) stress test in the Sun & Xu sense — there is no swapped-rubric-vs-name experiment here. **Noted as absent, per task instructions.**

## Stated limitations (Section "Limitations," verbatim-paraphrased with quotes, line 332–344)

"We evaluate one proprietary JEV version against a chosen set of configurations. Training overlap and benchmark contamination are unknown. Reasoning effort, model size, context limits, age, and serving systems all differ, so this is not a compute-matched architectural comparison." Model/version drift: "some service aliases and preview models can change; the earlier GPT quality runs and the expansion are labeled as separate windows, and one repeated JEV anchor cannot expose every model's drift over time."

"Decision-only prompts leave out generated explanations, explanation faithfulness, extended deliberation, and many grading rubrics. Verbalized probabilities and native distributions arise from different mechanisms. Invalid judgments count as errors in accuracy while probability scores condition on validity; the two denominators must not be conflated." "The local non-thinking Qwen settings do not stand in for the unavailable Groq endpoints, and PairRM has a shorter context and no rubric."

"The expansion followed inspection of the original study, and every multi-family analysis is exploratory. The extension is disjoint from policy fitting but shares benchmark families and was not newly hidden from the analyst. Small calibration and diagnostic sets give uncertain estimates; with many comparisons and descriptive ECE, no isolated superiority claim is supported, and a narrow interval on a saturated control says nothing about broad reliability."

"The existing annotations have incomplete provenance, the trajectory sample contains only reference-correct answers, and the synthetic controls are easy. The human adjudication in Appendix I covers the 183 items selected by judge disagreement, not a random audit of the benchmarks, and it is not consensus at scale: one human annotator completed every item, an LLM rater's second pass served only to select items for adjudication, and the adjudicator is an author who had seen aggregate but not item-level results. RewardBench preferences remain partly subjective (human–LLM κ = 0.29), and 36 of the 183 final labels are indecisive. Benchmark labels are unchanged in every primary result; human-corrected accuracies are a sensitivity analysis." "Specialized professional domains are untested."

"The RewardBench 2 and RM-Bench checks compare JEV, GPT-6, and Skywork on small balanced samples, with GPT-6 added in a later window on unchanged inputs. RewardBench 2 excludes Ties and uses four-way selection, so its score is not a binary RewardBench 1 accuracy. Skywork is one modern reward model, its raw scores carry no probability interpretation, and other architectures could change the comparison. Known benchmark performance and unknown training overlap limit any claim of independent out-of-distribution validation."

"Latency reflects one client location, collection window, pacing, and provider load. Bulk timings suffered client-accounting contention and are not pooled with the isolated panel; a 120-item panel estimates tails poorly, and serial model groups still see service variation over time. Fees follow documented cache rules with missing usage bounded conservatively; there are no invoices, energy measurements, or API-equivalent prices for local GPUs. Simulated cascade fees say nothing about real cascade latency, and the single-order cascades of Section 7 are post hoc, their thresholds read off the items they are scored on; only the frozen two-order policies test a pre-specified rule."

(Ethical considerations, line 346–348, adds: the only new human judgments are the 183 blinded adjudications by research-team members, no crowd workers; API credentials/account metadata excluded from the manuscript package; "Confident errors argue against using any automated judge as the sole arbiter of consequential decisions.")
